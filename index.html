About

I started out on the engineering side of machine learning, building things like RAG chatbots and forecasting models. Somewhere along the way, I got more curious about why models behave the way they do than about just getting them to work, and that curiosity pulled me into research.

My first real research project was my undergraduate thesis, where I looked at how easily language models can be tricked into saying things they shouldn't. I spent months throwing jailbreak prompts at different LLMs, which turned into my first paper at ICECTE 2026. That work led me to build Dynamic Gated Distillation, a way to make small models safer without making them worse at reasoning.

After graduating, I moved into vision-language models at AMIR Lab. There I built MGQ-Former, a model that answers questions about satellite images while holding on to the small regional details most models tend to blur out. It reached 78.3% accuracy on EarthVQA.

These days, I'm most excited about agentic AI: systems that don't just answer, but first decide where to look and which sources to trust. I'm exploring this in genomics, building agents that reason directly over raw DNA sequences.

If any of this overlaps with what you're working on, I'd love to hear from you.

Research Focus

01

LLM Safety & Jailbreak

How LLMs fail under jailbreak prompts, and post-training methods like knowledge distillation that make smaller models robust while keeping their reasoning intact.

02

Agentic AI & RAG

Retrieval systems that route each query to the right source based on what that source can actually do, with reasoning that resists hallucination.

03

Multimodal Learning

Vision-language fusion and Q-Former style architectures for visual question answering, especially where fine-grained regional detail matters.

04

AI for Genomics

DNA language models for promoter and enhancer classification, and agents that reason over raw genomic sequences.
